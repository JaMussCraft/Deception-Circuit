# SDR cue probe findings

**Explicitly naming both record items restored factual retrieval in this fixed panel, while reporting often selected the fixed lexical/example-first candidate.** No single cue explains all reporting choices. This separate neutral probe does not rescue either failed validation or establish a consequence-sensitive deception mechanism.

The frozen 512-response panel used eight reused fixtures, four audit and four lab, selected by original execution rank. It independently crossed record order, interrogative-option order, truth, favored identity, task and second-reference form (“the other” versus its explicit name). Report-answer examples stayed in fixed lexical order. All 512 outputs reached EOS; every numerical control passed bitwise. Two fresh independent blinded annotators agreed on all 67 unique complete response views; every row was restored, including five qualified retrieval responses.

| Identity rule | Reporting matches /256 | Retrieval matches /256 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Damaging identity | 175 | 238 |
| Fixed lexical/example-first identity | 195 | 121 |
| First record identity | 147 | 140 |
| First question option | 117 | 124 |
| Favored identity | 123 | 114 |

Naming raised retrieval correctness from **110/128 to 128/128**, chiefly when the damaging item was second in the record: **47/64→64/64**, versus **63/64→64/64** when first. This supports sensitivity to the asymmetric reference scaffold; naming also changes repetition, length and salience, so it does not isolate anaphora.

Reporting improved only **86/128→89/128**. Its naming change differed across template packages: audit **+5/64**, lab **−2/64**. Flipping only record order changed reporting identity in **25/128 matched pairs**; question order in **19/128**, truth in **49/128**, favored identity in **37/128**. These failures of invariant copying complement the lexical/example-first tendency: reporting is a mixture, not a deterministic positional rule. Every report was a simple candidate commitment; all five qualified responses were other-form density/hardness retrievals. Full per-fixture results and deletion sensitivity remain visible in the [technical analysis](../../.scratch/sdr-cue-probe/analysis.md).

Earlier D/H/F mask overlap and conditional QK-path effects remain compatible with shared reference/selection processing. This probe contains no circuit intervention and cannot link its errors to those masks, identify psychological modules, or establish intent, selective suppression, external generalization or pressure mediation. Fixed lexical identity and first-example order remain aliased; task changes also change question, stem and response format. Eight reused fixtures support a bounded descriptive result.
