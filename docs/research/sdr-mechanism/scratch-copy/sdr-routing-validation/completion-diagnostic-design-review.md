## Scientific purpose and boundary

The reported baseline contains 1,280 frozen prompts with passing bitwise numerical controls but a failed prospective neutral-retrieval gate: template A truth strata 38/64 and 40/64 versus template B 62/64 and 59/64. The coordinator reports all 512 fresh retrieval rows favor the true candidate over the alternative candidate, while 114 have non-item vocabulary argmax, often a prefix such as ` listed`, ` "`, ` the` or ` damaged`. Completing every original row can distinguish non-answer prefixes from a final factual answer. Positive candidate-relative margins alone do not establish full-vocabulary compliance, nor do plausible prefixes prove that a later answer will be correct.

Use **all the same 1,280 prompts**, same assistant stems, greedy decoding, at most 32 new tokens, no new lexical variants or row-specific retry. This is a post hoc baseline completion/format audit. It can describe final textual behavior under the frozen constrained stems. It is neither an independently confirmed capability test nor evidence that the earlier forced-choice gate actually passed. Any future confirmatory protocol would require a prospectively specified outcome and new design review; it is outside this run.

No selected-row extension beyond 32 tokens.

## Frozen labeling and analysis

Freeze the extraction/category rubric before model inference. Separate **expressed answer identity** from **factual correctness**, then join the extracted identity to the independently frozen record truth. Require a unique, affirmative final answer for deterministic identity assignment. Mere presence of one candidate token is insufficient when it appears in a negated statement, quotation, hypothetical, copied question, or an unfinished sentence. Both-name answers, self-correction, answer-plus-contradiction, no identifiable answer, refusal, format-only continuation and invalid text need explicit mutually exclusive categories or an explicit hierarchy. Refusal requires complete text conveying refusal, never non-item first-token argmax.

Ambiguous cases receive two independent labels blinded to stakes/favored/truth-factor assignments, baseline argmax, earlier gate outcomes and the expected mechanism. Give labelers the candidate names and full assistant text needed to identify the expressed answer. Reveal additional record/task context only where required by the frozen rubric and record that fact; answer identity should ordinarily be labeled before factual correctness is computed. Preserve each original label and adjudication. Unresolved disagreements remain unresolved unless the rubric predefines an independent adjudication step. All-row double labeling is acceptable if practical but not required to make a deterministic clear-case parser valid. Internal agents are authorized here; no external paid judges.

Report the full cross-tabulation of first-token item/non-item versus completed correct/incorrect/ambiguous/refusal/capped/unresolved, separately by fresh/native, template, task, stakes, favored alignment and truth identity as already frozen. Keep original first-token scores and the failed gate visible alongside the new descriptive endpoint. “Prefix-only failure” means an original non-item argmax followed by a completed unambiguous correct response; it is an observed descriptive category, not retrospective compliance with the old gate.

Keep complete fixture panels together for any exploratory bootstrap and preserve the original designed-fixture/pair-dependence caveats. ## Runtime and budget assessment

Worst case is **1,280×32 = 40,960 generated tokens**, or **160 batches×32 = 5,120 full-prefix forward batches** at physical batch 8/fixed length 192, before numerical receipts, decoding and artifact writes. EOS can reduce executed steps, but its frequency is unknown. A standard cached-generation tokens/second estimate does not apply to the specified uncached hook path.

The coordinator reports 191 actual seconds for the earlier baseline allocation. That total includes different work and cannot establish the completion runtime. Multiplying it naively by 32 gives approximately 6,112 seconds (102 minutes), a deliberately crude and potentially very pessimistic comparison, not a forecast. It illustrates why an assumed one-hour fit is unverified. Existing H200 pilot rates also do not measure this A100 completion runner.

Require a short fixed, outcome-independent calibration using the same computation and a conservative forecast including artifact/checksum overhead. Calibration rows retain their manifest identity; do not use their text to tune prompts, cap or rubric. If the one-hour reservation cannot contain the fixed grid plus shutdown margin, obtain a sufficient allocation under existing user authorization and operator accounting, or return an explicit incomplete all-row diagnostic. Do not silently drop rows, extend only promising rows, switch cache/scoring semantics or borrow old reservations to make a nominal budget fit. Failed reservations count; actual runtime does not replenish reserved budget.

**Release conclusion:** the diagnostic is scientifically appropriate for distinguishing formatting prefixes from final factual errors and describing full baseline output, with the restrictions above. Keep the original failed gate and stopped causal-validation stage unchanged regardless of completion results.
